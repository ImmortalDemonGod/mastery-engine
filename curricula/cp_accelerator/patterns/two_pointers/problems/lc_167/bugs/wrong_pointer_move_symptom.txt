# Bug Symptom: Wrong Pointer Movement in Two Pointers

## What You'll Observe

Your two-pointer solution returns an empty list (no solution found) for most inputs,
even though a valid pair exists.

## Expected Behavior

- `twoSum([2, 7, 11, 15], 9)` → `[1, 2]`
- `twoSum([2, 3, 4], 6)` → `[1, 3]`

## Actual Behavior

- `twoSum([2, 7, 11, 15], 9)` → `[]` (pointers diverge instead of converging)
- `twoSum([2, 3, 4], 6)` → `[]`

The pointers move in the wrong direction: when the sum is too small, the right
pointer moves left (making it smaller), and vice versa.

## Debugging Guide

1. **Trace the first iteration**: For [2, 7, 11, 15] with target 9:
   - sum = 2 + 15 = 17 > 9. Which pointer should move? Why?
2. **Think about monotonicity**: Moving left RIGHT makes the sum ___. Moving right LEFT makes the sum ___.
3. **Key insight**: To DECREASE the sum, move the RIGHT pointer left (replace the large number). To INCREASE, move LEFT right.

## Conceptual Understanding

The two-pointer technique relies on sorted order to make directional decisions.
When the sum is too large, we need a SMALLER sum → move right pointer left.
When the sum is too small, we need a LARGER sum → move left pointer right.
Swapping this logic makes the pointers diverge instead of converge.
